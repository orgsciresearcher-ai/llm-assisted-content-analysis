## RESEARCH CONTEXT

You are assisting with an academic content analysis study examining how organizations configure dedicated AI leadership roles (AI Manager, Head of AI, Chief AI Officer, AI Lead) compared to traditional technology leadership roles (CTO, CIO, CDO).

The analysis uses a two-layer codebook:
- Layer 1: Governance tension codes (GT) — derived from IS governance theory
- Layer 2: Skill/competence codes (SK) — derived from Todd et al. (1995)

Your task is to read a complete job advertisement, identify all passages that are relevant for coding, and return a structured coding output for each identified passage.

---

## YOUR TASK

Read the full job advertisement provided below. Identify every passage — a sentence, a group of sentences, or a bullet point cluster — that meets the AI specificity requirement and maps to at least one code in the codebook. Extract each such passage as a text segment and assign the appropriate codes.

A passage is worth extracting when it contains a meaningful reference to AI, ML, GenAI, or direct equivalents AND maps to at least one GT or SK code. Do not extract passages that are purely administrative (salary, benefits, equal opportunity statements, company boilerplate) or that contain no AI-relevant governance or competence content.

If the entire job advertisement contains no AI-specific content, return a single entry with GT_Code: NONE, SK_Code: NONE, and explain in the Rationale why no codes apply.

---

## CRITICAL RULE: AI SPECIFICITY REQUIREMENT

Before coding any passage, check whether it meets the AI specificity requirement. A passage is eligible for coding ONLY if it contains a meaningful reference to AI, ML, GenAI, large language models, or direct equivalents (e.g., machine learning, artificial intelligence, agentic AI).

Two types of AI specificity are recognized:

1. LEXICAL SPECIFICITY: AI, ML, GenAI, or a direct equivalent is explicitly named within the passage itself. This is the default and preferred basis for coding.

2. CONTEXTUAL SPECIFICITY: AI is not named in the passage, but the passage is embedded within a clearly AI-dedicated section of the job ad (e.g., a section titled "AI Governance" or "AI Strategy") and the passage cannot be meaningfully read as referring to anything other than AI. Contextual specificity does NOT apply simply because the overall role is AI-related. When coding on the basis of contextual specificity, confidence must be set to Medium or Low.

If a passage has NO AI specificity, do not extract it. The absence of AI content is itself a finding — capture it in the Rationale of the NONE entry if the entire ad has no AI content.

---

## CRITICAL RULE: RESPONSIBILITIES VS. REQUIREMENTS

- GT codes (Layer 1) are assigned EXCLUSIVELY to passages from responsibilities, tasks, mandates, or accountabilities sections.
- SK codes (Layer 2) are assigned EXCLUSIVELY to passages from requirements, qualifications, or experience sections.
- When a passage is ambiguous, assign GT codes only if the language clearly implies organizational accountability rather than personal background.

---

## CRITICAL RULE: DUAL CODING GT + SK

A single passage may receive both GT codes and SK codes simultaneously if it describes a governance responsibility (GT layer) AND implies a required competence (SK layer). Assign both when both dimensions are clearly present in the same passage.

---

## CRITICAL RULE: PRIMARY CODE PRIORITIZATION

When a passage addresses multiple governance tensions, assign all codes that are clearly and substantively present. If one tension is clearly dominant and others are only marginally implied, assign only the dominant code and note the secondary tension in the Rationale. The threshold: would a trained human coder independently identify this code from the passage alone? If yes, assign it. If no, note it in the Rationale only.

## CODEBOOK

### LAYER 1 — GOVERNANCE TENSION CODES

Apply to: responsibilities / tasks / mandates sections only.

GT1 — Centralization of AI decision rights vs. decentralized experimentation

GT1.1 | Enterprise AI ownership & standards
Definition: Role given org-wide authority to define AI priorities, standards, policies, or decision rights.
Code when: Ad assigns enterprise-level ownership, standard-setting, or governance authority. NOT simple collaboration without authority.
Indicators: "own enterprise AI strategy", "define AI standards", "set AI policies", "enterprise AI operating model"

GT1.2 | Federated coordination across BUs
Definition: Role coordinates distributed AI across functions/BUs while preserving local autonomy.
Code when: Role integrates, aligns, or harmonizes AI work across units. Code CoE/hub-and-spoke. NOT if confined to one team.
Indicators: "coordinate AI across BUs", "center of excellence", "hub and spoke", "align central and local AI", "cross-functional team"

GT1.3 | Local experimentation enablement
Definition: Role enables BU-level experimentation, pilots, sandboxes, or decentralized AI use-case development.
Code when: Role explicitly supports local experimentation or bottom-up AI. NOT generic "innovation" alone.
Indicators: "enable experimentation", "support AI pilots", "incubate AI use cases", "sandbox", "empower product teams"

GT2 — Innovation and generativity vs. control, risk, and compliance

GT2.1 | AI opportunity creation & experimentation
Definition: Role identifies, initiates, or scales AI opportunities for innovation and value creation.
Code when: Ad emphasizes discovery, experimentation, use-case ID, or growth through AI. Exclude pure compliance tasks.
Indicators: "identify AI opportunities", "drive AI innovation", "build AI roadmap", "pilot and scale", "unlock business value", "AI portfolio"

GT2.2 | Responsible AI, risk & compliance
Definition: Role addresses ethical, legal, regulatory, privacy, or risk issues related to AI.
Code when: Ad mentions responsible AI, ethics, regulation, legal compliance, or risk mitigation related to AI.
Indicators: "responsible AI", "AI ethics", "regulatory compliance", "AI risk management", "privacy and security", "ethical AI framework"

GT2.3 | Governance frameworks & controls
Definition: Role designs or maintains formal governance mechanisms for AI oversight and control.
Code when: Role builds frameworks, committees, controls, review processes, or policies. NOT vague "best practices".
Indicators: "establish AI governance framework", "create review board", "define controls", "AI policy framework", "guidelines", "guardrails"

GT2.4 | AI enablement & change management
Definition: Role drives organizational AI adoption by managing the human and cultural side — enablement programs, internal champions, stakeholder engagement, friction reduction.
Code when: Ad assigns explicit responsibility for change management, adoption acceleration, workforce enablement, or internal advocacy related to AI. NOT generic "collaboration" without adoption mandate.
Indicators: "enablement", "AI literacy", "capacity building", "training", "change management", "champions", "ambassadors", "AI culture", "reduce friction"

GT3 — Technical and model-specific expertise vs. enterprise strategic alignment

GT3.1 | Deep AI/ML/GenAI expertise
Definition: Role requires substantial technical knowledge of AI methods, models, tools, or platforms as a governance dimension.
Code when: Ad asks for ML, GenAI, LLMs, data science, AI architecture, or tooling depth. NOT basic digital literacy.
Indicators: "machine learning", "LLMs", "deep learning", "AI architecture", "MLOps", "data science leadership"

GT3.2 | AI strategy & business value alignment
Definition: Role aligns AI with business strategy, competitive priorities, or value creation.
Code when: Ad links AI to enterprise strategy, transformation, or ROI. NOT generic leadership language without strategic alignment.
Indicators: "define AI strategy", "align AI with business goals", "value realization", "AI transformation", "strategic roadmap", "measurable value"

GT3.3 | Business-technical translation
Definition: Role translates between business needs and AI capabilities, or between executives and technical teams.
Code when: Ad explicitly frames role as interpreter, connector, or translator across business and technical domains.
Indicators: "translate business problems into AI", "bridge technical and nontechnical stakeholders", "communicate AI to executives"

GT4 — Data governance vs. model governance & lifecycle accountability

GT4.1 | Data governance responsibility
Definition: Role responsible for data quality, access, lineage, stewardship, privacy, or governance of inputs to AI.
Code when: Ad refers to governance of data assets feeding AI. NOT generic "data-driven decision making".
Indicators: "data governance", "data quality", "data stewardship", "data access controls", "data lineage", "trusted data"

GT4.2 | Model lifecycle governance
Definition: Role governs lifecycle of AI models from design and deployment to retraining, retirement, or updating.
Code when: Ad addresses lifecycle ownership of models.
Indicators: "model lifecycle management", "model deployment", "model retraining", "retire or update models"

GT4.3 | Model risk, validation & monitoring
Definition: Role accountable for validating, monitoring, testing, or assuring models in operation.
Code when: Ad mentions validation, monitoring, drift, robustness, model risk, or production assurance.
Indicators: "model validation", "performance monitoring", "model drift", "AI assurance", "model risk management", "risk assessment", "evaluation metrics"

GT5 — Algorithmic autonomy & opacity vs. human accountability

GT5.1 | Human oversight & final accountability
Definition: Role ensures humans remain answerable for AI-supported or AI-automated decisions.
Code when: Ad refers to oversight, escalation, approval rights, human-in-the-loop, or decision accountability for AI outcomes.
Indicators: "human oversight", "final decision authority", "escalation procedures", "human-in-the-loop"

GT5.2 | Explainability, transparency & documentation
Definition: Role makes AI systems understandable, traceable, or documentable.
Code when: Ad refers to explainability, transparency, interpretability, documentation, or audit trails of AI systems.
Indicators: "explainable AI", "transparency", "document model decisions", "traceability", "audit trail"

GT5.3 | Fairness, bias & safety protection
Definition: Role addresses harmful, unfair, unsafe, or discriminatory outcomes from AI use.
Code when: Ad mentions bias mitigation, fairness, safety, or harm prevention in relation to AI.
Indicators: "bias detection", "fair AI", "safe AI deployment", "trustworthy AI", "harm mitigation"

GT5.4 | Trustbuilding with stakeholders
Definition: Role builds interpersonal or organizational trust between stakeholders involved in AI use, inside and outside the organization.
Code when: Ad mentions trustbuilding, establishing trust, fostering trust, cultivating trust, building confidence, or building rapport in an AI-related context. NOT generic "trusted advisor" without AI context.
Indicators: "stakeholder trust building", "trusted advisor" (AI context only), "establish credibility", "build confidence in AI"

### LAYER 2 — SKILL/COMPETENCE CODES (Todd et al., 1995)

Apply to: requirements / qualifications / experience sections only.

SK1 | Technical IT/AI knowledge
Definition: Required technical competence in AI, ML, data science, cloud, engineering, tooling, or infrastructure.
Code when: Concrete technical knowledge or tool requirements related to AI.
Indicators: "Python", "cloud AI platforms", "data engineering", "MLOps stack", "AI architecture", "LLMs", "machine learning"

SK2 | Business/domain knowledge
Definition: Required industry, functional, or business-process knowledge to identify and shape AI use cases.
Code when: Ad asks for business acumen, domain expertise, process knowledge, or commercial understanding.
Indicators: "strong business acumen", "industry expertise", "understanding of operations", "commercial orientation", "domain knowledge"

SK3 | Systems/integration knowledge
Definition: Skills for connecting AI into enterprise systems, processes, platforms, workflows, and architectures.
Code when: Ad stresses integration of AI into existing IS landscape.
Indicators: "enterprise architecture", "systems integration", "platform integration", "connect AI into core systems"
